Today's session

    The streak counts days where you finish at least 3 of the first 6 items.

    Why the session is built this way

    1. Ladder volume on its own plateaus. At a high level you already know the game. What's left to gain is in the few turns per game you get wrong, and you only find those by logging them and looking at them later.

    2. Daily drills build automatic knowledge. Type matchups, speed tiers, items and mechanics should be instant, so your thinking time goes to reads and planning. Five minutes a day of drills keeps it sharp, and missed questions come back more often.

    3. One focus per week. When you try to fix everything at once, nothing changes. Pick one habit and run it every game until it's automatic. The weekly review shows you which one to pick next.

    4. Plan before turn 1. Most best-of-1 losses are decided at team preview. Saying your bring-4bring-3, lead and win condition out loud makes the decision deliberate, and your log shows whether the plan was the problem.

    5. Review a loss turn by turn. You remember the critical hit, not the turn 2 Protect that set it up. Rewatching finds the turn the game actually turned.

    6. Predict top players. Calling a top player's bring-4bring-3 and leads before they're revealed trains the read you need at preview.

    7. Best of 3 every week. Ladder is best of 1, but big events are best of 3. Games 2 and 3 are about adapting to what you saw, and you can't practice that on ladder.

    Games

    Fast, scored games for the knowledge that has to be instant in a real battle: speed, turn order, damage and type matchups. Every game ends by reviewing what you missed, with the real numbers.

    How to use this page
    1. Pick the format and which Pokémon to play with. Top 50 in the meta drills exactly what you'll face.
    2. Start on the easier level of a game, then move up when you're scoring well.
    3. Read the review at the end of every game. That's where most of the learning happens.
    4. One game counts as your daily study drill on the Today page.

    Test

    An exam over everything in Study: type matchups, speed, priority, items, abilities, mechanics and common sets. No feedback until the end, so it measures what you actually know.

    How to use this page
    1. Difficulty: Standard checks the basics. Hard uses close calls, look-alike answers and tricky mechanics. Expert adds exact Speed numbers, abilities that change type matchups, and questions like "which move does it NOT run". Harder tests count for more in your ratings.
    2. Take a Full test now to set a baseline. Retake it every couple of weeks to measure real improvement.
    3. Use a Quick test when you're short on time, and an area test to dig into your weakest area.
    4. At the end you get a grade, a breakdown by category, your rating changes, and a review of every question you missed.
    5. Every test updates your ratings on the Progress page.

    Progress

    Your skill ratings over time, from every game and test. Ratings account for difficulty, so they show real improvement even as you move to harder levels.

    How to use this page
    1. Each area starts at 1000. Right answers raise it and misses lower it. Beating harder questions counts for more.
    2. A rating is provisional until it has 10 answers. Early ratings move a lot, then settle.
    3. Ranks: Novice under 1000, Trainer 1000+, Ace Trainer 1200+, Expert 1400+, Master 1600+, Champion 1800+.
    4. Your overall rating averages the areas that aren't provisional. Study drills don't count, because they repeat your misses on purpose.
    5. Use the buttons on each area to train it, then check back here.

    Battle companion

    Keep this open on your phone next to your Switch. It counts field turns, tracks what your opponent reveals, and turns the game into a log entry when it ends.

    How to use this page
    1. Pick the format, then at team preview tap each slot to add their 6 and tap Start battle.
    2. Tap Next turn at the start of every turn. Timers count down on their own.
    3. When Tailwind, Trick Room, weather, terrain or screens go up, tap it. The card shows how many turns are left, counting the current one.
    4. On each opponent card, record what they reveal: item, ability, moves. Tap Protected when they Protect, and the card warns you when a second Protect in a row is likely to fail.
    5. When the game ends, tap Won or Lost. The log form opens already filled in.

        How Worlds invites work

        2027 season rules
        • Each season runs from September to that year's Worlds in August. Championship Points reset every season.
        • Winning a Regional gives a Worlds invite plus 350 Championship Points.
        • Your best 5 Regional and International finishes count. Locals count too: League Challenge 1st place earns 15 Championship Points and League Cup 1st place earns 50, with your best 4 of each counting.
        • Online Global Challenges also award Championship Points, counted by rating zone.

        These rules change between seasons. Check the current numbers before you plan around them: Victory Road season structure.

        2027 season events: North America and majors

        Treat these as reps, not deadlines

        Every big event you play, even a 3–4 finish, is best-of-3 experience against strong players. Find League Challenges and Cups near you with the Play! Pokémon event locator. Dates are from Victory Road's calendar as of Nov 12, 2026, so check them before booking travel.

        Replay review

        Step through a Pokémon Showdown replay one turn at a time. See what happened and everyone's HP, mark the turn the game swung, and write what you'd do differently.

        How to use this page
        1. On Showdown, press Upload and share replay after a game and copy the link.
        2. Paste the link here and press Load replay. Pick which player you were.
        3. Step through the turns with the arrows (or your keyboard's arrow keys). Stop where the game turned and press Mark as the key turn.
        4. Write a note on any turn: what you'd do now. Add a one-line takeaway at the end.
        5. Save review keeps it here. Log this game sends the result, teams, key turn and notes to the game log.

        Team sheet practice

        At official events both players see each other's full team sheet before the match. Practice that here: read an opponent's sheet against your team, choose what to bring and lead, write a plan, then review it.

        How to use this page
        1. Pick one of your saved teams.
        2. Pick an opponent: a random top-cut team, a specific one from Top teams, or paste any team.
        3. Read both sheets and the matchup notes: speed order, who threatens whom, and tools to watch for.
        4. Tap your Pokémon to choose what you bring and lead. Do the same for what you expect from them. Write your plan.
        5. Save plan. Afterward, come back and note what actually happened, or save the plan to your Notebook.

        Account, backup & theme

        Your account keeps your data safe and the same on every device. You can also download backups and choose how the app looks.

        How to use this page
        1. Sign in with the same username and password on your phone and computer. Your data syncs between them on its own.
        2. Everything also saves on the device, so the app works offline and syncs when you're back online.
        3. There's no email on accounts, so write your password down. A forgotten password can't be reset.
        4. Download a backup now and then. It's one file you can restore anywhere.

        Your account

        Advanced: sync through a GitHub gist instead
        Only needed if you don't want to use an accountNot set up

        Your data saves on this device automatically. To use the same data on your phone and computer, connect a GitHub token. The app keeps your data in one private gist on your GitHub account, and nothing else on your account is touched.

        1. On github.com, open Settings → Developer settings → Personal access tokens → Fine-grained tokens, and choose Generate new token.
        2. Name it "Toxicroak VGC Lab". Under Account permissions, set Gists to Read and write. Leave everything else off.
        3. Generate it, copy it, and paste it below. Do the same on each device. They'll all find the same gist.
